# HSync user guide

HSync is a general-purpose logic analyzer app. It drives DreamSourceLab DSLogic devices, ALIENTEK ATK-Logic devices (DL16 and DL16 Plus), PXLogic PX-Logic devices (PX-Logic 32, 16 Pro, 16 Plus and 16 Base), Sipeed SLogic devices (SLogic Combo 8, SLogic16U3 and SLogic32U3) and a built-in demo device. This guide walks through the interface in the order you use it. Installing HSync is described in the [README](../README.md#download).

## Interface

From top to bottom, the window has a top bar, the work area and a status bar; the data stream opens on the right of the work area:

- **Top bar**: the device, the acquisition settings (sample rate │ duration │ channels), the trigger and the capture button; on the right, the search box, export, the data stream button and settings.
- **Overview strip**: a thumbnail of the whole capture. A thin band for each decoder marks where it has data, coloured like its decode blocks, with its errors in red; the outlined box is the current view.
- **Timeline**: the ruler, and the channel and decode rows grouped by decoder. Channels not assigned to a decoder go into the **Other channels** group.
- **Data stream** (optional): on the right, a list of all the decoded results of one decoder, with every field of the event you select below it.
- **Status bar**: capture status, trigger time, sample count, segment measurement and zoom buttons.

## Devices

- HSync starts with an empty timeline; waveforms appear once you capture or open a file.
- Click the device button at the left end of the top bar to choose a device. Without hardware, HSync opens the **Demo device**; click **Capture** and it plays back the bundled protocol demo data (I²C, UART, SPI, CAN-FD), with its decoders already set up the first time, plus two analog channels: a 1 kHz sine wave from 0 to 3.3 V on A0 and a 3 kHz one of ±2.5 V on A1.
- The first time you open a DSLogic, HSync loads the firmware and FPGA bitstream onto it automatically; this takes a few seconds.
- An ATK-Logic is listed as **ATK-Logic** until it is opened, then by its model, such as **DL16 Plus**. HSync does not update its firmware: if it reports that the firmware needs an update, update it with ALIENTEK's ATK-Logic app, then plug the device in again.
- A PX-Logic is likewise listed as **PXLogic** until it is opened (except the first 32-channel devices, named at once), then by its model, such as **PX-Logic 16 Plus**. HSync loads its FPGA bitstreams each time it opens it. If the device runs another firmware version than the one HSync brings (PXView 1.6.5's), HSync first writes its own into the device, which restarts and is opened again; this takes a few seconds more.
- A Sipeed **SLogic Combo 8**, **SLogic16U3** or **SLogic32U3** uses the firmware already installed on the device; HSync does not update it. See [Sipeed SLogic](#sipeed-slogic) below for connection details and limits.
- If you plug in a logic analyzer while the demo device is open, the status bar shows "… connected"; click **Switch** to use it. If a loose USB cable makes the device drop out briefly and it reappears within two seconds, HSync reopens it automatically and restores its settings.
- If a USB 3.0 model is plugged into a USB 2.0 port, the status bar reminds you to move it to a USB 3.0 port; otherwise Stream mode's speed is limited.
- On Linux, every program shares one pool of memory for USB transfers, 16 MiB unless raised, and HSync keeps its own transfers within three quarters of it. If a capture still stops with "Not enough memory for USB transfers", close other programs that use USB devices directly, such as another analyzer application, or raise the pool, for example with `echo 64 | sudo tee /sys/module/usbcore/parameters/usbfs_memory_mb`.
- The device's options sit in the acquisition settings (below): the **Operation mode** (**Buffer mode** or **Stream mode**), the channel mode (as the channel tiers), **Threshold voltage** (**Threshold level** on models with fixed thresholds), the input filter (**Filter one-sample glitches**), **RLE compression**, the external clock and its edge (**Use an external clock**, **Sample on the falling clock edge**), the **External trigger input** and, for Buffer mode, what happens **When stopped**. Each device lists only the options it has: an ATK-Logic has the operation mode and a **Threshold voltage** from −5 to 5 V; a PX-Logic has the operation and channel modes, a **Threshold voltage** from 0 to 6 V, the filter, the clock edge and the **External trigger input**. An SLogic has Stream mode and a **Threshold voltage** from 0 to 6 V, initially 1.7 V; its channel tiers follow the highest enabled channel and the USB link.
- HSync remembers the settings of each kind of device and restores them the next time you open it: the device options, enabled channels, triggers, channel colours and capture mode at once, the channel names and decoders with the first capture after it opens. Showing a file or another device's capture leaves the names and decoders remembered for this device alone; removing every decoder is remembered too.

### Sipeed SLogic

Connect the analyzer directly to the computer, close any other application using it, then select **SLogic Combo 8**, **SLogic16U3** or **SLogic32U3** from the device menu. The SLogic16U3 needs a USB 3.0 port and data cable; the SLogic32U3 reaches its full rates only on a 10 Gbps USB port with a 10 Gbps cable, not through a hub or dock. On Windows they need WinUSB; on Linux install the SLogic udev rule in the [README](../README.md#first-launch). HSync recognizes the devices with USB IDs `359f:3031` and `359f:3032`; bootloader mode is not supported.

HSync uses the firmware installed on the analyzer and does not flash it. The SLogic32U3 needs firmware S/N 202610150528 or newer; to check or update it, follow [Sipeed's firmware guide](https://wiki.sipeed.com/hardware/en/logic_analyzer/slogic32u3/Hardware_Specification.html#Firmware-Update).

For the **SLogic Combo 8**, switch to logic-analyzer mode (blue indicator) before connecting; its USB IDs are `359f:0300` and `359f:3000` on newer firmware. USB 2.0 is its native link and does not cause a slow-port warning. Its input threshold is fixed, so acquisition settings offer no voltage control. Its debugger and serial-port modes are outside HSync. No custom firmware or firmware toolchain is needed.

The Combo 8 rate tiers are D0–D1 up to 160 MHz, D0–D3 up to 80 MHz and D0–D7 up to 40 MHz, with discrete rates from 1 MHz. These follow the current official driver, whose 160 MHz mode exceeds the original 80 MHz product specification; actual sample timing and sustained throughput still need real-device validation in HSync. Command traces and simulated captures cover all three wire widths.

For a first capture, connect the analyzer's ground and one channel to a known low-frequency digital signal, select 5 MHz and a short duration, leave the trigger unset and press **Capture**.

An SLogic captures in Stream mode, including Single, Repeat, Loop and Instant. The highest enabled channel determines the sampling limit, even if only one channel is enabled, and so does the USB link:

| Enabled channels | SLogic16U3 on USB 3.0 | SLogic32U3 at 10 Gbps | SLogic32U3 at 5 Gbps |
| ---------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------- |
| Only D0–D3       | 800 MHz               | 1400 MHz              | 800 MHz              |
| Only D0–D7       | 400 MHz               | 800 MHz               | 400 MHz              |
| Any of D8–D15    | 200 MHz               | 400 MHz               | 200 MHz              |
| Any of D16–D31   | —                     | 200 MHz               | 100 MHz              |

On USB 2.0 the SLogic16U3 is limited to 80, 40 and 20 MHz; Sipeed does not support capturing with the SLogic32U3 on USB 2.0. Choose from the discrete rates shown in the acquisition settings. For example, enabling only D15 still has the 16-channel limit. On the U3 models the adjustable threshold is shared by all channels. Simple edge and level triggers are evaluated in software; stages, serial triggers, Buffer mode, RLE, external clocks and external trigger inputs are unavailable. The pre-trigger history uses at most 64 MiB (16,777,216 samples), so long captures can contain less history than the requested trigger position; the trigger marker shows the actual position.

With the SLogic32U3's optional ADC module connected, each group of eight inputs (D0–D7, D8–D15, D16–D23, D24–D31) can be read as one 8-bit analog channel, A0 to A3, over ±15 V. In the acquisition settings, click **Analog** at the end of a row of channel numbers: the row turns into **A0 · analog ±15 V**, and those eight inputs are captured as that analog channel instead of as logic channels. The other rows stay logic channels, so analog and logic channels are captured together. While an analog channel is on, the sample rates are those of the ADC module's 800 MHz clock from 100 MHz up (100, 160, 200, 400 and 800 MHz, as the channels allow), and turning one on moves a slower rate up to 100 MHz. Analog channels take no trigger conditions, and their waveforms appear under **Analog channels** (see [Viewing waveforms](#viewing-waveforms)).

## Acquisition settings

Click the sample rate │ duration │ channels button in the top bar. The pop-up goes from top to bottom in the order the settings depend on each other:

- **Operation mode**: **Buffer mode** stores the capture in the device's memory and uploads it afterwards; it reaches the highest sample rates, but the device's memory limits the duration. **Stream mode** sends samples to the computer while capturing; this computer's memory limits the duration, and USB bandwidth limits the sample rate. Switching mode keeps the duration as close as possible to what it was.
- **Channels**: on most analyzers, fewer channels allow faster sample rates. The row above the channel numbers shows these tiers for the current mode, with the current tier highlighted. A tier named by a range, such as **0–3 ≤800 MHz**, **0–7 ≤400 MHz** and **0–15 ≤200 MHz** on an SLogic16U3, depends on the highest enabled channel: the device sends every channel up to it, so enabling only channels 0 and 14 still uses **0–15**, and a line below the channel numbers says so. A tier named by a count, such as **6 ch ≤500 MHz** on a DSLogic, takes any that many channels. Click a tier to use its channels, starting from channel 0; hover over it to see which channels it takes. On a device whose channels do not limit the sample rate, such as the demo device, the row offers channel counts instead. **All** and **None** enable or disable every channel. Click a channel number to enable or disable it; enabling a channel the current tier cannot take moves to the next tier that can, which lowers the highest sample rate.
- **Sample rate**: every rate of the operation mode is listed. A rate that needs fewer channels is greyed out and says what it needs, such as **≤4 ch** or **0–3 only**; choosing it moves to that tier and keeps the enabled channels the tier allows.
- **Duration**: durations are listed in a 1-2-5 sequence. When you change the sample rate or the channels, the duration stays as close as possible to what it was. In Stream mode the first entry is **Until stopped**: the capture runs until you click **Stop**, or until it has filled half of this computer's memory (or reached the device's own limit). Fewer channels make that longer.
- In Buffer mode, durations longer than the device memory can hold are marked **RLE**. Choosing one turns on RLE compression automatically (on a DSLogic, **RLE compression** must be allowed in the device options beside the lists). A compressed capture fits only while the signals change slowly enough; when they change too often, the capture stops and the status bar says so. Devices without RLE compression, such as a PX-Logic, list only the durations their memory holds.
- A line below the lists says what limits the duration, such as "Device memory holds about 1.31 s of 4 ch" or "This computer’s memory holds about 21.5 s of 16 ch". If the selected capture needs more than half of this computer's memory, the pop-up warns you to shorten the duration or use fewer channels.
- Beside the lists (below them in a narrow window) are the device's other options, listed under [Devices](#devices). While a capture runs they cannot be changed.

## Capturing

- Click **Capture** to start, and click it again (it now reads **Stop**) to end. The status bar shows the progress, such as "Waiting for trigger" and the percentage captured.
- Use the arrow to the right of **Capture** to choose how to capture:
  - **Single**: capture once.
  - **Repeat**: capture again a set time after each capture completes. With **Repeat** selected, set the interval under **Repeat every** in the same menu (0.1–10 s, default 1 s). From the second capture on, the screen keeps the previous complete result while the new one is captured in the background, and the new one replaces it in one go once complete. By default, stopping midway discards the new capture and keeps the previous one; to show the latest data instead, turn on **Show the latest data when a repeat stops** in the settings.
  - **Loop**: capture continuously, keeping only the latest stretch (as long as the selected duration); when you stop, that last stretch is shown. Available only on devices that can loop (a DSLogic, an ATK-Logic or a PX-Logic in Stream mode, an SLogic, or the demo device).
  - **Instant**: ignore the trigger conditions and start at once.
- Successive captures with the same device keep the current zoom and position, so you can keep your eye on the same detail across repeated captures.
- When the computer cannot take in the data fast enough, the status bar reports that data was lost ("USB bandwidth exceeded; some data was lost").

## Triggers

- **Simple trigger**: each channel row has a trigger slot at the right end of its label. Click it to choose **None**, **Rising edge**, **Falling edge**, **Any edge**, **High level** or **Low level**. When several channels have conditions, the trigger fires only when all of them hold at once. The **Trigger** button in the top bar sums up the current conditions.
- Click **Trigger** in the top bar to open the trigger settings:
  - **Position**: the share of the whole capture that comes before the trigger point (fixed at 1% in Stream mode on DSLogic, ATK-Logic and PX-Logic; adjustable up to 90% on an SLogic, subject to its pre-trigger history limit).
  - **Stages** (a DSLogic in Buffer mode): up to 16 stages. Each stage has two patterns, **Pattern A** and **Pattern B**; you can **Invert** each one and **Combine** them as **A and B** or **A or B**. Once a stage has held the set number of times (**Count**; tick **In a row** to require consecutive matches), the trigger moves on to the next stage.
  - **Serial** (a DSLogic in Buffer mode): **Start**, **Stop** and **Clock** conditions, the **Data** channel, and a data **Value** of up to 16 bits (each bit 0, 1 or X).
- **External trigger input** (a PX-Logic): in the device options, choose the edge or level on the device's trigger input that fires the trigger (**Rising edge**, **High level**, **Falling edge**, **Low level** or **Any edge**), or **Off** to ignore the input. An **Instant** capture ignores it too.

## Viewing waveforms

- **Zoom**: Ctrl (⌘ on macOS) + wheel, or pinching on a trackpad, zooms around the pointer; to zoom with the wheel alone, turn on **Zoom the waveform with the wheel alone** in the settings (over the channel labels on the left, the wheel then still scrolls the channels). You can also use the −, + and **Zoom to fit** buttons in the status bar, or the `+`, `-` and `Home` keys. Each notch of a mouse wheel zooms by the same step and the view glides there smoothly, as do the zoom buttons and keys and the `←` / `→` keys; turning several notches quickly adds them up. A trackpad follows your fingers directly. With reduced motion turned on in the system, the view moves at once.
- **Zoom limits**: once fully zoomed in or out, further zooming in the same direction leaves the waveform in place. Any zoom animation already in progress finishes normally.
- **Pan**: drag in the waveform area; use Shift + wheel or swipe sideways on a trackpad; or press `←` and `→` (with Shift, half a screen at a time). If you let go after a quick drag, the waveform keeps gliding for a moment (you can turn this off in the settings).
- **Overview strip**: click to centre the view there, drag to move the view, and use the wheel to zoom.
- **Hover readout**: rest the pointer on a waveform and the pulse under it is shaded, while the status bar shows its channel and width, plus the period, frequency and duty cycle it makes with the next pulse. Data lines a decoder uses, such as SDA or MOSI, show only the width; UART lines show roughly how many bits the pulse spans instead. To read it beside the pointer instead, set **Readout position** to **Floating** in the settings (Appearance): the readout then floats just above the row in a semi-transparent box, so the waveforms behind it stay visible; the edge measurement's result floats above its line the same way.
- **Edge measurement**: click an edge on a waveform to start measuring. When the pointer comes near another edge, on the same channel or another, it snaps to it, and the status bar shows the time between the two edges, the number of samples and 1/Δt as you move. Click again to finish; the measurement stays on the waveform and its result in the status bar. Click another edge to start a new measurement, or click an empty spot or press `Esc` to clear it. While measuring you can still drag, pan with the wheel and zoom, so if the end point is off screen, move there first and then click.
- **Segment**: drag along the ruler to set points A and B, or click a decode block to make its start and end the segment. Zoom in to mark a short stretch; a segment can be as short as one sample. The status bar shows the segment's duration, exact to one sample like an edge measurement; click it to see each channel's rising edges, falling edges and edge count within the segment. Press `Esc` to clear the segment.
- **Channels**: click a channel's colour dot, double-click its name or right-click it to rename it or change its colour.
- **Waveform height**: drag the bottom edge of a channel's label to make its waveform taller or shorter; double-click the edge to give it back its default height. Like colours, heights are saved in sessions and `.hsync` files.
- **Fitting the window**: the waveforms grow and shrink with the window, keeping their proportions, so that they fill the timeline: up to three times their height in a tall window, and in a short one down to the height of the narrow rows before the channels scroll. Dragging an edge then makes that waveform taller while the others make room. To keep fixed heights instead, turn off **Fit waveform heights to the window** in the settings (Behaviour).
- The arrow at the start of a group header expands or collapses the group.
- **Analog channels** are listed last, under **Analog channels**, on taller rows drawn like an oscilloscope's screen: four divisions, a line at 0 V, and the volts per division under the channel's name. Zoomed in far enough, every sample shows as a dot on the waveform. The scale fits the channel's samples by default. To change it, hold Alt (⌥ on macOS) and turn the wheel over the row (the volts under the pointer stay put), or hold Alt (⌥) and drag the row up or down to move the waveform; the + and − beside the scale, shown while the pointer is over the label, change it too. **Fit** puts the scale back to fit the samples. Resting the pointer on an analog waveform shows its voltage there; when zoomed out so that a pixel holds a range of voltages, the readout gives the range.

## Protocol decoding

- Click **Add decoder** at the top left of the timeline and search for a protocol. HSync decodes with its own engine, uiharu: buses (I²C, UART, SPI, CAN, USB, I²S, JTAG, SWD, MDIO, Microwire, 1-Wire, PS/2, Wiegand…) and signal lines (PWM, timing, parallel, WS281x, AM230x, infrared…). Protocols that read another one's output — the 24xx EEPROM on I²C, SPI NOR flash and SD cards on SPI, Modbus RTU and LIN on UART — are chosen under **Stack on it**. Choose a channel for each line the protocol reads, set the options and, if you like, give the decoder a name.
- **Flash and SD**: choose **SPI / QSPI NOR flash** for opcode, address, dummy and data phases, with explicit SPI/QPI and SDR/DTR settings for the supported Winbond profile. **Dual / quad SPI** remains available for fixed-width SDR lane groups. **SD card (commands and data)** decodes CMD traffic, one- or four-lane reads and writes, CRCs and busy periods; configure the initial bus width or enable negotiation from captured initialization traffic. DSView imports select a fixed width from the mapped data pins; review that assumption when the capture includes a width change.
- **Debug and trace**: choose **JTAG**, then **JTAG chain selection** when the capture contains multiple TAPs, and stack **Arm JTAG-DP** and **Arm MEM-AP** for debug accesses. Configure the known chain widths; captured requests and prior responses are distinct observations. STM32F1 and MIPS EJTAG have separate profiles. UART trace bytes can feed the trace transport and then ITM or ETMv3. **STM8 SWIM (sync and recovery)** needs the initial target clock and a known command boundary or captured reset. Optional synchronization calibration trusts the next complete low pulse after entry/reset as the target response; enable it only when the capture establishes that assumption. DSView imports keep calibration disabled.
- **Device interpretation**: stack SMBus on I²C for a selected transaction format and PEC checking, or the keyboard/mouse profile on PS/2. RTCs, sensors, GPIO expanders, converters, memories and RF parts appear under their compatible bus. Some SPI devices require one-bit words rather than byte words; their protocol pages specify the framing. Select the actual chip variant and reference voltage where applicable. TLC5620 can observe LOAD/LDAC directly; without LOAD, explicitly enable word alignment only when the capture begins on a word boundary. The [coverage record](decoder-coverage.md) links each supported profile and its limits.
- **Compact debug and 1-Wire devices**: cJTAG reconstructs the same JTAG stream used by the chain and debug layers. Let it observe activation, or explicitly assert an aligned initial OScan0/1 packet. Stack **Arm SWD DP/AP** on SWD for posted reads and candidate MEM-AP addresses; these observations do not prove that a memory write succeeded. DS2408, DS2432/DS2433 and DS28EA00 stack on the 1-Wire network layer. CFP stacks on MDIO, whose sampling edge is configurable. AM230x engineering values stack on the raw sensor decoder.
- **UART semantic profiles**: MIDI at 31,250 baud, 8N1 can feed the MIDI semantic layer for note/controller names and derived values. DMX512 timing and slots reads UART at 250,000 baud, 8N2; zero-slot filtering and timing thresholds are configurable. LIN version and idle profile reads 8N1 at its configured baud, selects the version's checksum and uses an explicit idle threshold to finish responses. A capture ending before that threshold stays incomplete.
- Add compatible layers repeatedly under **Stack on it**, for example **1-Wire link layer → 1-Wire network layer → DS18B20**. Each layer has its own options. Removing a layer also removes its dependent upper layers. Editing a saved chain preserves its node identities and exposed outputs; complex graphs keep their connections intact while supported options and display settings can be edited.
- Imported DSView decoder configurations are retained with the capture, including unsupported decoders, stacked layers and options. Expand **Imported decoder notes** above the overview to see what could not be translated. Supported portions run; unsupported entries stay saved without running. Saving and reopening an `.hsync` capture preserves the original settings. **Remove retained decoder** explicitly removes the selected entry.
- Decoding follows the capture: a running capture already shows what has been decoded, and decoders can be added or changed while it runs.
- Every event a decoder finds becomes a block on the timeline, named after its kind (address, data, frame…) with its main value; hovering shows every field. Click the settings button on a group header (**Decoder settings**) to change or remove a decoder; under **Display** you can choose the number base (**Hex**, **Dec**, **Oct**, **Bin** or **ASCII**).
- A block's colour says what the event is, the same in every protocol: blue for an address (an I²C address, a CAN ID), violet for a command, green for a frame's control field, teal for data, magenta for a CRC or checksum, olive for the other side's answer (an acknowledge, a handshake), grey for where a transaction begins or ends, grey stripes for a condition of the line itself (a line reset, a break), and red for an error — including a wrong parity bit, a CRC that does not match or an address no device answered.
- Blocks prioritize the data carried on the bus, such as JTAG's TDI/TDO, over scan numbers, offsets and bit counts. Single-sample events such as scan boundaries appear as thin markers; a block shows its text whenever it fits, a narrower one keeps its colour, and nearby events merge into a dense block coloured by what most of them are. Errors never merge with other events and stay visible however far you zoom out. Zoom in or use the **Data stream** to inspect them. Events with exactly the same span share a marker whose hover details list each event.
- **JTAG**: the **TAP states** lane separates states from shift data, coloured by the register they belong to: violet for the instruction register, teal for the data register, grey for reset and idle, with the shift states darker; on the data lane, each scan's begin and end markers and its shifts take the colour of the register it shifts. Each state extends until the next state change, stopping at any data gap or the current decoding progress. `Shift-IR` means shifting the instruction register; `Shift-DR` means shifting the data register. State names follow the interface language. Hover for the complete name and duration, or click to select its whole interval. The data stream still records the exact instant each state was entered.
- **Data stream**: click the data stream button at the right of the top bar, between export and settings, or the data stream button on a group header (**Show all decoded results in the data stream**). It opens on the right of the timeline; drag its left edge to make it wider or narrower. It lists one stream at a time, one row per event: its time, the number of the transfer it belongs to (such as an I²C transaction or a JTAG scan) in the **#** column, its kind and its fields, labels in grey and values bright; choose another decoder or stream from the menu at its top left, and switch between **All** and **Segment**. Click a row to jump to that place on the waveform and select the event: every field of it, with its time and length, shows in full below the list, however much the row has to cut short. Clicking a decode block on the timeline selects its event the same way, and with the list focused, ↑ and ↓ step to the event before or after. You can **Copy** the list or **Export** it as CSV or text. To put it on another screen, click **Open in its own window** at the top right of the panel: the data stream moves into a window of its own, which you can place anywhere and resize, and the waveforms get the whole main window back. HSync remembers this, so the data stream button opens that window from then on; **Put back in the main window** in the window brings the panel back beside the timeline. Everything works across the two windows: click a row and the main window shows it, hover a row and it is marked on the timeline, and searches and segments in the main window show in the list.
- **Your own decoders**: a decoder written in HSync's decoder language (see [uiharu/docs/dsl.md](../uiharu/docs/dsl.md)) runs in the app without a rebuild. Compile it with `pnpm dsl:compile`, then open **Settings → Decoders** and click **Load module…** to choose the `.json` file it wrote: the decoder appears under **Add decoder** like the built-in ones, and a module named like a built-in decoder (`spi`, `uart`…) stands in for it while it is loaded, so you can try a change to a built-in decoder on a capture at once. Loaded modules are remembered and loaded again at startup; **Remove** lets one go. A module the app cannot load stays listed with the reason. A module shows under the names its author gave it in the DSL (`locales`, in Chinese and English or whatever languages it declares), falling back to the app's own copy for a decoder of that name and then to the identifiers. A saved `.hsync` capture carries the modules its decoders use: opened on another machine, it brings them along, listed under **Settings → Decoders** with the capture file as their origin until they are removed or the app is restarted.

## Search

Press `/` to focus the search box in the top bar:

- **Decoded data**: type a number (`79` or `0x4F`) to find the events with a field of that value, several numbers (`0x4F 0x32`) to find the events whose bytes hold them in a row, or a word (`address`, `frame`, `stop`) to find events of that kind or with that enumerated value; every word must match. Press Enter for the next match, Shift + Enter for the previous one, and `Esc` to clear the box.
- **Level pattern**: type conditions such as `D3=R D5=1` (0 low, 1 high, R rising, F falling, C either edge, X any), then press Enter to find the next place where they all hold.

## Files

- **Open**: **Open file…** in the device menu, or ⌘O / Ctrl+O, reads HSync's own `.hsync` captures, which come back with the decoders (and the decoder modules they use), channel names, colours, heights and view they were saved with, and DSView `.dsl` files (all versions). Choosing a VCD, FST or GHW waveform instead, as simulators and FPGA logic analyzers (Vivado's ILA, Quartus' Signal Tap, …) export them, opens the **Import waveform** dialog.
- **Import**: the dialog lists the file's scopes and signals as a tree; tick the signals to import (search them by name), and click **Import**. Every bit becomes a channel, so a vector `data[7:0]` gives `data[0]` to `data[7]`; signals without a logic level (reals, strings) cannot be imported, and unknown (`x`) or high-impedance (`z`) values read as low. The **Sample rate** starts as the file's timestamps give it: the coarsest grid every timestamp falls on, which gives a logic analyzer export its sampling clock back when enough signals change; type the clock the file was sampled at if you know it, such as `25 MHz`. A line under the tree shows the sample rate, the number of samples and channels, and the memory the import needs. An imported capture has no decoders; add them as usual. **Save capture…** writes it as an `.hsync` file.
- **Save**: **Save capture…** in the export menu (the download icon in the top bar), or ⌘S / Ctrl+S, saves an `.hsync` file: HSync's own format, holding the samples (compressed), the channel names, the decoders and the loaded decoder modules they use, the channel colours and heights and the view, which all come back when the file is opened; the device settings of the moment are stored in it too. To give a capture to DSView, export it as a DSView capture: the `.dsl` file opens in DSView with its channel names and keeps HSync's decoders for HSync, and supported decoder settings from DSView files are translated (I²C/SPI/UART with supported device profiles, CAN, QSPI, native SD and selected debug/display links). Original settings and unmapped options remain saved with import notes. A saved UART number format (hexadecimal, decimal, octal, binary or ASCII) becomes the entry’s number display setting. Flash and EEPROM display choices remain attached to their original layer: a choice that conflicts with the entry’s single display format is retained with an import note, rather than changing every lane. Unsupported format values remain visible in the saved import metadata.
- **Export**: **Export…** in the export menu writes an HSync `.hsync` or DSView `.dsl` capture, CSV, VCD, FST, sigrok `.sr` or raw binary. You can export just the segment, or only some of the channels. CSV exports quote channel names containing commas, quotes or line breaks so each name stays in one column. You can cancel a save or an export while it runs.
- **Sessions**: **Save session…** in the export menu stores the device settings, channels, triggers, decoders and capture mode as `.hsync-session.json`; **Load session…** in the device menu also reads DSView `.dsc` sessions. A session loaded before anything was captured keeps its channel names and decoders for the first capture.

## Settings

The **Settings** button at the right end of the top bar (or ⌘, / Ctrl+,) opens the settings page; pick a section on the left:

- **Appearance**: **Theme** (**System**, **Light** or **Dark**), **Density** (**Comfortable** or **Compact**), **Language** (**System**, **简体中文** or **English**) and **Graphics acceleration**: with **Auto (WebGPU)**, waveforms are drawn with WebGPU, falling back to Canvas2D automatically where WebGPU is unavailable; the page shows which one is in use.
- **Behaviour**: **Centre the view on the trigger**, **Follow the latest data while capturing**, **Keep scrolling after a quick drag**, **Zoom the waveform with the wheel alone**, **Show the latest data when a repeat stops** and **Check for updates at startup**. The first three have no effect while the view is zoomed to fit.
- **Shortcuts**: every keyboard shortcut, grouped as **File**, **View** and **Capture**. Click an action's box and press the keys of the new shortcut together, such as ⌘⇧S or Ctrl+Shift+S (up to four keys); `Esc` cancels. **×** clears a shortcut, the arrow at the right of a row restores that action's default, and **Restore all defaults** restores them all. Giving an action a shortcut that another one had moves it, and the row says from where. **Start or stop capturing**, **Show or hide the data stream**, **Export** and **Open the trigger settings** have no shortcut until you give them one.
- **Diagnostics**: the **Log level**, whether to **Write a log file**, and **Show in folder**, which opens the folder that holds the log.
- **About** (at the foot of the side bar): the version and license, and updates. Click **Check for updates**; when a new version is available, click **Download and restart**, and once the download finishes the app restarts into the new version. With **Check for updates at startup** on, a small dot appears on the settings button and on **About** when a new version is found.

Opening menus and panels, switching options, and adding or removing list items all come with short animations. If you turn on reduced motion in your system's accessibility settings, the interface stops playing them.

## Keyboard shortcuts

These are the defaults; change them in **Settings › Shortcuts**. Shortcuts without ⌘ or Ctrl do nothing while you type in a text field.

In the desktop app, F5 and Ctrl/⌘+R do not refresh the interface. Browser actions such as saving a web page, printing, browser find, history navigation and whole-page zoom are disabled in both the main window and the separate data stream window, even when an application shortcut is cleared or unavailable. Ctrl/⌘+S saves the capture when its default binding is in use. You can assign browser key combinations to application actions in the shortcut settings. Right-click opens application menus where available; text fields keep their normal editing menu and copy/paste shortcuts.
