# WS281x

The `ws281x` decoder reads the frames of LED data on the data line of a chain of WS2812B-class LEDs (WS2812B, SK6812, SK6812RGBW and their kin) from logic samples and emits them as `ws281x.events`. It follows the WS2812B datasheet (Worldsemi) and the SK6812 and SK6812RGBW datasheets (OPSCO Optoelectronics): one data line at 800 kbit/s; every bit begins with a rising edge and lasts 1.25 µs ± 600 ns, a 0 high for 0.4 µs then low for 0.85 µs, a 1 high for 0.8 µs then low for 0.45 µs (each ± 150 ns); a low of 50 µs or more (80 µs or 280 µs on newer parts) resets the chain, which latches the data it received.

```rust
let leds = graph.add(Ws281x::new(Ws281xConfig { data: 0, ..Ws281xConfig::default() })?);
graph.connect(logic.samples, Ws281x::input(leds))?;
let frames = graph.expose("leds", Ws281x::events(leds))?;
```

## Configuration

`Ws281xConfig`, checked by `Ws281x::new`:

| Field          | Default | Meaning                                                                                 |
| -------------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `data`         | 0       | The data line's channel                                                                 |
| `bits_per_led` | 24      | The bits each LED takes: 24 for RGB parts, 32 for RGBW parts                            |
| `threshold_ns` | 625     | The high time that tells a 0 from a 1, in nanoseconds, at least 1: a longer high is a 1 |
| `reset_us`     | 50      | The low time that resets the chain and ends a frame, in microseconds, above zero        |

The default threshold lies halfway through the 1.25 µs bit, between the 0.4 µs and 0.8 µs highs. With parts that want a longer reset (80 µs, 280 µs), a low between 50 µs and that time ends a frame for the decoder but not for the LEDs: set `reset_us` to the part's value to group the data as the chain latches it.

The input port `logic` requires of the source `channels >= data + 1` and `rateHz >= ⌈2 × 10⁹ / threshold_ns⌉` (3.2 MHz for the default): with the threshold shorter than two ticks the highs of a 0 and a 1 cannot be told apart, so the graph refuses the connection, for example `ws281x#0.logic requires rateHz >= 3200000 of logic#0.samples, which declares 1000000`. That is a floor, not a recommendation: a high is measured to within a tick, so with the default threshold typical bits (highs of 0.4 µs and 0.8 µs) want at least 6 MHz, and bits at the edges of the datasheets' tolerance (0.55 µs and 0.65 µs) over 40 MHz.

## The stream: `ws281x.events` v1

Frames are numbered from 1. A frame is a `frame-begin`, one `led` per LED, and a `frame-end`, all carrying the frame's id.

| Kind          | Fields                                   | Meaning                                                                                                                     |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `frame-begin` | `frame`                                  | The first bit of a frame rose, after a reset                                                                                |
| `led`         | `frame`, `index: u32`, `bytes`           | The bits of one LED: `index` counts from 0 for the first LED the data reaches; `bytes` holds its 3 or 4 bytes in wire order |
| `frame-end`   | `frame`, `leds: u32`, `reason`           | The frame ended: `reset` when the low after its last bit reached the reset time, `aborted` when a gap or the end came first |
| `error`       | `frame`, `reason: "partial-led"`, `bits` | The reset came after `bits` bits that make no whole LED; it comes just before the frame's end, and the LEDs before it stand |

```json
{"kind":"frame-begin","frame":1}
{"kind":"led","frame":1,"index":0,"bytes":[255,0,16]}
{"kind":"led","frame":1,"index":1,"bytes":[18,52,86]}
{"kind":"error","frame":1,"reason":"partial-led","bits":5}
{"kind":"frame-end","frame":1,"leds":2,"reason":"reset"}
```

The bytes are reported as they are on the wire, the first bit received being the most significant bit of the first byte, without naming colours: the order depends on the part. On the WS2812B and the SK6812 an LED's 24 bits are green, red and blue (GRB); on RGBW parts such as the SK6812RGBW the 32 bits are green, red, blue and white (GRBW). Other parts and drivers use other orders, so mapping the bytes to colours is the reader's business.

| Event         | Span                                                                                                               | Settled                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| `frame-begin` | the tick the frame's first bit rose                                                                                | that tick                   |
| `led`         | its first bit's rising edge … the computed end of its last bit                                                     | its last bit's falling edge |
| `error`       | the leftover bits, from the first's rising edge to the computed end of the last                                    | the reset tick              |
| `frame-end`   | empty, at the reset tick (the last falling edge + the reset time), or at the gap's start or the end of the samples | that tick                   |

The computed end of an LED's last bit is that bit's rising edge plus the LED's shortest bit time — the least ticks from one of its bits' rising edge to the next's, which a pause between bits does not stretch — and never before the tick after the last bit's falling edge. With steady timing the LEDs of a frame cover it end to end. An `led` is settled where its last bit's value became certain, so it shows up as soon as its last high ends, long before the frame does.

The output port `events` declares:

| Property     | Value    |
| ------------ | -------- |
| `bitsPerLed` | 24 or 32 |

## Decoding rules

- A bit begins at a rising edge, and its value is the length of the high that follows: a high of _h_ ticks is a 1 when `h × 10⁹ > threshold_ns × rate`, that is when it lasts longer than the threshold, and a 0 otherwise, `rate` being the source's samples per second. How long the line then stays low does not matter, as long as it rises again before the reset time.
- A low of at least `reset_us`, `⌈reset_us × rate / 10⁶⌉` ticks from the falling edge, is a reset. It ends the frame under way at the tick where the low reached that length, whatever told the decoder — the samples up to that tick, a block used up right there, or progress — so the events do not depend on how the samples were cut. A rising edge at that very tick already comes after the reset.
- The first rising edge after a reset begins a frame. Bits make LEDs of `bits_per_led` bits in the order they come.
- Bits left over when the reset comes are reported as a `partial-led` error with their count, just before the frame's end.
- The first sample of the stream and the first after a gap are never an edge, and the line may be in the middle of a frame there: the decoder only decodes once it has seen a reset, a low of the reset time measured from the first sample or from a falling edge after it. A frame under way at the start or after a gap is skipped; a capture that begins with the line idle for the reset time decodes its first frame.
- A gap or the end of the samples before the reset ends the frame `aborted`, at the gap's start or the end; the bits of an LED it cuts are dropped. A reset whose tick the samples reached before the gap or the end ends the frame as usual.
- Progress is passed on, held back to the first rising edge of an LED under way: after progress at tick _t_, no event begins before _t_.

## Limits

- One data line, the chain's input: what an LED passes on to the next is not decoded separately.
- No glitch filter: every high pulse is a bit.
- Highs, lows and bit times are not checked against the datasheets' tolerances; only the threshold and the reset time decide.
- Colours are not named and the brightness is not computed: the bytes are reported as they are.
- Parts with other timing, such as the 400 kbit/s mode of WS2811 drivers, need `threshold_ns` set between their two highs.
